What you get

Up to eight options, each as a PNG with a background and a transparent PNG:

  • the whole logo, with its background or on the colour with the most contrast;
  • the main part only, without a separate name line;
  • the top part, cut off at the bottom edge, for tall mascots;
  • a coin with a complete ring, for round badges;
  • crisper colours, for soft or photo-like logos.

Not quite right? Every option has an Edit button: an editor with an eraser, a restore brush, a magic wand for leftover gaps, a brush, a pen for curves and shapes, with the 40 px result shown live.

Each option is checked against FIRST's rules (40 × 40 px, at most 20 kB, no colours near lime or magenta, which video overlays key out) and shown at actual size on FIRST's backgrounds. FTCScout shows avatars at 40 and 20 px.

  3. Upload at least 2 weeks before your event. Changes can take up to 24 hours to show.
  4. One submission at a time, and FIRST may not review more than three a season. See FIRST's avatar rules.

Questions

What size is an FTC team avatar?
FIRST takes a 40 × 40 pixel PNG of at most 20 kB. FTCScout and the event screens show it at 40 and 20 pixels, so it has to read at that size.
Which colours should a FIRST avatar avoid?
Colours close to lime green or magenta. Video overlays at events key those colours out, so those pixels can disappear on the stream. The avatar maker counts them in every option.
Should I upload the transparent or the opaque version?
Both follow FIRST's rules. The opaque one has a background colour; the transparent one has a thin border in that colour so it stays readable on any background. Pick the one that still reads at 20 pixels.
How do I upload a team avatar to FIRST?
Lead Mentor 1 or 2 logs in at ftc-scoring.firstinspires.org, chooses Team Avatar, then Upload New Avatar, and picks one PNG. Upload at least 2 weeks before your event: changes can take up to 24 hours to show. There is one submission at a time, and FIRST may not review more than three a season.
Does the avatar maker keep my logo?
No. Your browser turns the logo into a PNG and sends it once to make the options; the server keeps nothing. Edits are saved in your browser only, and as a project file if you download one.
Can a script or an AI assistant make the avatar?
Yes. The page offers its steps and the editor as tools to AI agents (WebMCP, or window.ftcEmbedsAgent), and the API behind it takes a logo and answers the options: see the avatar maker's docs and the project file format. Links like /avatars?team=23452&logo=https://… fill the page in.
